# Console models

Blender scripts for the console theme's own models (`godot/themes/console/`), and for memory-card's game case templates (below). Each script's docstring says what it builds and how to run it (`blender -b --factory-startup --python tools/console-models/<script>.py -- <args>` from the repo root; the print and video scripts with Python). Add or change a card's paragraph here in the same piece of work as the card.

## memory-card's game case templates

- Mega Drive clamshell (`mega_drive_clamshell.py`: `models/templates/megadrive_clamshell.glb`, the case's outer hull with its rims and hanger tab, sized by ScreenScraper's scans; no inputs)
- Switch case (`switch_case.py`: `models/templates/switch_case.glb`, one closed solid with the art as its own faces; no inputs)
- PlayStation 2 and PSP clamshells (`playstation_clamshell.py <ps2|ps2_blue|psp> <out>`: `models/templates/ps2_clamshell.glb`, black, `ps2_clamshell_blue.glb` and `psp_clamshell.glb`, grey; one hull in each case's proportions, one closed solid with its rounded edges, flat spine, seam and finger recess, its insert sized by ScreenScraper's scans)
- PAL Dreamcast jewel case (`dreamcast_jewel_case.py <images> <out>`: `models/templates/dreamcast_jewel_case.glb`, one closed solid of a CD case's face and 19 mm deep, the blue tray's hinge band down the front's left in a pocket under the lid, Dreamcast in its moulded panel, the lid's seam round the other sides, its art's faces sized by ScreenScraper's PAL scans; its one texture, the band and the plastic's swatches, from `dreamcast_jewel_case_print.py <images>`, both reading `dreamcast_jewel_case_layout.py`, which has the references)
- PAL Mega-CD and North American Sega CD cases (`sega_cd_case.py <megacd|segacd> <images> <out>`: `models/templates/megacd_case.glb`, the PAL double jewel case, 142 x 125 x 16.5 mm, and `segacd_case.glb`, the long box, 144 x 207 x 21.5 mm, each one closed solid, a clear lid over the front insert, a black tray, the lid's seam, its art's faces sized by ScreenScraper's scans; their one texture of swatches from `sega_cd_case_print.py <images>`, both reading `sega_cd_case_layout.py`, which has the references)

How each case is built, its measurements and its references (moved from docs/ARCHITECTURE.md A7 on 2026-11-03):

**The Mega Drive clamshell (2026-10-09).** `megadrive_clamshell` is Sega's plastic case (the European Mega Drive's and the US Genesis's) as one solid, built in Blender by `tools/console-models/mega_drive_clamshell.py` rather than by `BoxTemplateGenerator`, whose extruded outline can't make its rims or its tab. Its outer hull follows a reference model of the case: a flat spine wall; on the top, the bottom and the opening side, the two halves' walls set back 1 to 1.8 mm between a 1.2 mm rim round each face, with a seam where they meet; and the shop hanger tab, the back's rim carried 15.4 mm above the top, with its hook hole (a slot with a round notch over its middle). Nothing inside is modelled. Its paper insert is sized by ScreenScraper's scans, not a ruler: a 484 × 680 front and back and a 96 × 680 spine make it 131 × 184 × 26 mm, and 3 mm of the black case shows round it at the top, the bottom and the opening side, as on a real case, so the case is 134 × 190 × 26 mm (205.4 mm high with the tab). The `cover`, `back` and `spine` slots are the insert's faces (the front's and back's reaching round the 0.6 mm rounded edges to the spine), their UVs projected flat across the insert; the rest is black `case`. Every face is flat-shaded, as the generated boxes are: smooth normals bent towards the rounded edges spread over the long triangles across a face and showed as hot spots. 602 triangles, four materials, no texture.

**The PlayStation 2 clamshell (2026-10-18).** `ps2_clamshell` (black) and `ps2_clamshell_blue` are Sony's PlayStation 2 game case as one closed solid, built in Blender by `tools/console-models/playstation_clamshell.py` (`ps2` or `ps2_blue`), which builds the PSP's too (below): the case's outer hull, nothing inside it. It's no DVD keep case, and its shape follows the owner's photos of PAL cases (and a photo of an open case on Wikimedia Commons for the recess's ends): from the front, a rectangle with rounded corners (2.5 mm by the spine, 4 mm by the opening side); its edges round the front and the back rounded (1.5 mm) at the top, the bottom and the opening side, and crisp (0.3 mm) along the spine, where the clear sleeve folds round it, blending from one to the other round the spine's corners, so the spine is flat; the seam where the two halves meet, 0.5 mm wide and 0.4 mm deep in the middle of the depth, round the top, the opening side and the bottom, stopping where the spine's corners start; and in the opening side the finger recess, a slot with 2 mm rounded corners from 50 to 140 mm up, 3 mm deep, leaving a 1 mm flat rim beyond each rounded edge, the seam running along its floor and up its ends. Its paper insert is sized by ScreenScraper's PAL scans, not a ruler: a 486 × 680 front and back and a 55 × 680 spine (the scans' usual sizes; a few backs are 483 wide, cropped by 0.6%) make it 130.8 × 183 × 14.8 mm, and 3.5 mm of case shows above and below it and 4.2 mm by the opening side, so the case is 135 × 190 × 14.8 mm (a real one is about 14 mm deep). The `cover`, `back` and `spine` slots are the hull's own faces: the insert's, the front's and the back's reaching round the spine's edges, their UVs projected flat across the insert, as on the Mega Drive clamshell. The rest is `case`: black (`#111114`), PS2's, or the blue of the later PAL cases in the owner's photos (`#2A55B5`). Until the owner's first look the spine's edges were rounded like the others: the spine's art and the cover's or the back's met on a 1.5 mm curve, which showed as strips and made the spine look curved inwards. The flat faces are flat-shaded, and only the rounded edges and corners are smooth (an edge is smooth when it joins two faces at under 50°, neither of them square to the case), as on the Switch case. 564 triangles, four materials, no texture.

**The PSP clamshell (2026-10-18).** `psp_clamshell` is Sony's PSP game case, the PlayStation 2 case's hull in the PSP's proportions, built by the same script (`psp`) with the same corners, edges, seam and recess. The recess runs from 40 to 145 mm up, from a retailer's photo of an open case (Mediaxpo's replacement case, sold by checkoutstore.com, which also gives 104 × 178 × 15 mm). Its insert is sized by ScreenScraper's scans: a 398 × 680 front and back (a few backs are 395 wide, cropped by 0.75%) and a 59 × 680 spine make it 100.7 × 172 × 14.9 mm, with 4 mm of case above and below it and 3.3 mm by the opening side, so the case is 104 × 180 × 14.9 mm. A real case is clear plastic; its `case` is a light grey stand-in (`#A4A8AE`, roughness 0.25), as the launcher draws no transparency. 579 triangles, four materials, no texture.

**The Dreamcast jewel case (2026-11-02).** `dreamcast_jewel_case` is the case PAL Dreamcast games came in, as one closed solid, built in Blender by `tools/console-models/dreamcast_jewel_case.py` (its measurements in `dreamcast_jewel_case_layout.py`, its texture from `dreamcast_jewel_case_print.py`). It has a CD jewel case's face, 142 × 125 mm, with 1 mm corners and a 0.6 mm chamfer round the front and the back, but it's 19 mm deep, and its tray is an opaque, strong blue (`#1B417A`): the tray's hinge band shows down the front's left, 12.3 × 122 mm, a pocket the clear lid stands 1 mm proud of, between a 1.2 mm clear wall at the case's edge and the lid's hinge knuckles at its ends, "Dreamcast™" in a rounded panel moulded into it, read from the bottom up; round the top, the opening side and the bottom, a 0.4 mm seam 3 mm back from the front, where the lid meets the base. Its references: photographs of PAL cases on The Dreamcast Junkyard (one empty, showing the tray and the band, and two closed, straight on, which give the band's width, the panel's place and the lettering's length, set in Segoe UI, close to the case's), the owner's reference pictures for the colours (a photograph of a closed case straight on and a studio render of one at three-quarters), and 300 dpi scans of PAL inserts on the Internet Archive (Maddog's), which give their real size: a 120.2 × 119.5 mm booklet and a 151.7 × 118.6 mm inlay with one spine, on the hinge side. The art is sized by ScreenScraper's PAL scans of the owner's library (two thirds of its 235 Dreamcast games had them: a 640 × 640 front, a 784 × 680 back and an 87 × 680 spine; North American and Japanese ones have a 791 × 680 back and a 39 × 680 spine, a CD jewel case's), so none of it is cropped: the `cover` slot is the booklet under the lid, 120 mm square, 18.4 mm in from the left edge and 2.5 mm from the top and the bottom; the `back` slot the inlay's back seen through the base, 136.7 × 118.6 mm, centred; the `spine` slot the inlay's spine seen through the base's hinge side, 15.2 × 118.6 mm, from 1 mm in front of the back edge. The depth isn't measured: it's the inlay's spine and what a CD jewel case has round its own (10.4 mm less a 6.5 mm spine). Everything else is `case`, whose colours come from one 256 × 1024 texture: the band as the front shows it (its blue, the panel a little darker with a light rim, the lettering in near-black), and a 128-pixel swatch each for the other faces' colours, their UVs at its middle (constant over a triangle, so always sampled at full resolution, as the arcade cabinet's palette is): the clear lid and base with the tray behind them (`#485A78`, darker and greyer than the band), the clear plastic's edges and the lid's flange in front of the seam (`#A9B6C0`), and the seam (`#26334A`). The case's roughness is 0.3, the art's 0.22, under the clear plastic. Every face is flat-shaded. 756 triangles, four materials, one texture. Rendered, the band comes out a strong blue, hue 217° and saturation 0.7, as the owner's photograph has it (hue 220°, saturation 0.68), between the photograph and the render in brightness, and the lid round the cover darker and greyer, as the photograph's is. The first colours, a slate blue matched to a daylight photograph, came out too light and muted beside the owner's pictures.

**The Mega-CD and Sega CD cases (2026-11-03).** `megacd_case` is the case PAL Mega-CD games came in, and `segacd_case` the long box North American Sega CD games came in, each as one closed solid, built in Blender by `tools/console-models/sega_cd_case.py` (`megacd` or `segacd`; their measurements in `sega_cd_case_layout.py`, their texture from `sega_cd_case_print.py`). Both are the Dreamcast jewel case's kind of hull without its band: 1 mm corners, a 0.6 mm chamfer round the front and the back, a clear lid over the front insert and a black tray behind it, a 0.4 mm seam 3 mm back from the front round the top, the opening side and the bottom (from 3 mm in from the hinge side), and the inlay's one spine seen through the base's hinge side, 0.5 mm in front of the back edge. Their art is sized by ScreenScraper's scans of the owner's Mega-CD library, all 680 pixels high, so none of it is cropped: the `cover` slot is the front insert, centred on the front; the `back` slot the inlay's back, centred on the back; the `spine` slot the spine. The scans are the measurements' main source, since neither case's size is published:
- **`megacd_case`**, 142 × 125 × 16.5 mm: Sega Retro calls the PAL case "the jewel case quad variation", one half holding the multilingual manuals, and sellers of originals and reproductions a double jewel case. An 801 × 680 front, a 799 × 680 back and a 73 × 680 spine, which at a jewel case inlay's 118 mm high are a 139 × 118 mm front insert covering the face nearly whole, a 138.7 mm back and a 12.7 mm spine. The scale is checked by the back's EAN-13, 183 of its 680 pixels long, which at its nominal 31.35 mm makes the scan 116.5 mm high. The depth isn't measured: it's the spine and what a CD jewel case has round its own, as the Dreamcast's.
- **`segacd_case`**, 144 × 207 × 21.5 mm: 20.7 × 14.4 × 2.1 cm, measured from an original on racketboy.com's forum, 0.5 mm deeper so the spine fits between the chamfers. An original back insert is 5 9/16 × 7 15/16 in (Sega-16's forum): 201.6 mm high, at which the 476 × 680 back scan is 141.1 mm wide, as the insert is; the 481 × 680 front is 142.6 mm and the 67 × 680 spine 19.9 mm.

Everything else is `case`, its colours from a 64 × 256 texture of four swatches, their UVs at their middles: the clear plastic with the black tray behind it (`#1E2024`), its edges, rims and the lid's flange (`#6C727A`, lighter, so the outline reads on a dark background), the seam (`#0B0C0E`) and the tray round the spine (`#141518`). The case's roughness is 0.3, the art's 0.22. Every face is flat-shaded. 572 and 588 triangles, four materials, one texture. Japanese Mega-CD games are in `jewel_case`: their scans are a CD jewel case's (a 680 × 680 front, a 792 × 680 back and a 37 × 680 spine).

**The Switch case (2026-10-07; one solid from 2026-10-11).** `switch_case` is Nintendo's Switch game case, 104.1 × 172 × 11.5 mm, built in Blender by `tools/console-models/switch_case.py`. It's one closed solid, the case's outer hull with nothing inside: from the front, a rectangle with 3.5 mm rounded corners; round its edge, a 0.8 mm chamfer on the front (1.1 mm deep) and on the back (2 mm deep); and in the opening side, the groove the two halves leave between them (from 45.7 to 126.3 mm up, 4.66 mm deep, its mouth's lips bevelled, its inner corners rounded). The `cover`, `back` and `spine` slots are the hull's own faces: the front's and the back's from the spine's chamfers to 2.7 mm short of the opening side, the spine's wrapping both chamfers, all from 5.1 to 167.7 mm up, so the light grey `case` shows at the top and the bottom. The front and the back are 100.6 × 162.6 mm, the shape of ScreenScraper's 396 × 640 box scans (528 of the owner's 1,170 Switch covers; the other box scans are within 1% of it), so a cover isn't cropped; until 2026-11-10 they ran from 6.5 to 166.3 mm, the hand-made model's, which cut about 1% off each cover's top and bottom. The spine's art spans 0.0613 of its height (`extras.aspect`), centred on the wrap, and the chamfers take its edges (its UVs clamped to 0..1). The flat faces are flat-shaded, and only the rounded corners are smooth. 236 triangles, four materials, no texture. Until 2026-10-11 it was a hand-made model whose measurements the script keeps: a hollow shell of 0.3 mm walls (516 triangles), with the art as quads laid 0.17 mm over it, the spine's replacing the wall it covered, which left a hole at each end of the spine.

## The cards

`game_gear.py <images> <out>` builds the Game Gear (`models/systems/gamegear.glb`: the black Game Gear standing up, three-quarter view, its whole lens the `video` material, a node tree whose `focused` clip drops a cartridge (a made-up game, Crimson Kunai) into the notch at the top of its back, slides POWER on, presses START and plays on the D-pad, 1 and 2, then switches off and takes the cartridge out) from the atlas and the lens's still `game_gear_print.py artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws, and `game_gear_video.py artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws the lens through the clip, frame for frame (`videos/gamegear.mp4`: the backlight coming up, a made-up publisher's splash, the title and the game); all three read the measurements, the atlas regions and the clip's timing in `game_gear_layout.py`, and `game_gear.py` keeps `rounded_polygon`, `rectangle` and `ellipse`, which other cards import;

`game_boy.py <images> <gb|gbc> <out>` builds the Game Boy and the Game Boy Color (`models/systems/gb.glb`, a grey DMG-01; `gbc.glb`, a Game Boy Color in Dandelion; standing up, three-quarter views, each lens the `video` material, each a node tree whose `focused` clip drops a cartridge (a made-up game) into the bay at the top of its back, slides the power switch ON, presses START and plays on the D-pad, A and B, then switches off and takes the cartridge out) from the textures `game_boy_print.py artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws (`<kind>_print.png`, the shell's faces; `<kind>_cart.png`, the cartridge's two sides; `<kind>_screen.png`, the lens switched off), and `game_boy_video.py artifacts/made-up-games <gb|gbc> <out>` draws the lens through the clip, frame for frame (`videos/gb.mp4`: the DMG's start-up and Kaleidrop in its four greens; `videos/gbc.mp4`: the Color's start-up and Ghoulish Gus); all three read the measurements, the atlas regions and the clip's timing in `game_boy_layout.py` (`game_boy_print.py`'s `Face`, `rgb`, `rounded_polygon`, `FONTS` and `SCALE` are other print scripts' too);

`gba.py <images> <out>` builds the Game Boy Advance (`models/systems/gba.glb`: the indigo AGB-001 standing up, three-quarter view, its back's middle swelling round the cartridge's bay, its lens the `video` material, a node tree whose `focused` clip drops a cartridge (a made-up game, Kaleidrop) in over the top, slides the power switch ON and lights POWER, presses START and plays on the D-pad, A, B and R, then switches off and takes the cartridge out) from the atlas and the lens's still `gba_print.py artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws, and `gba_video.py artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws the lens through the clip (`videos/gba.mp4`: the start-up, Kaleidrop's title and a round of its play); all three read the measurements, the atlas regions and the clip's timing in `gba_layout.py`;

`mega_drive.py` builds the Mega Drive (`models/systems/megadrive.glb`: the first one, the European one in black, from photographs of it, its `build()` the console alone (standing on its rubber feet, the seam between its halves round it, its control panel sunk a little into the slope, the slot's two dust flaps, the sockets in its back, its underside's screws, vents and labels; only the faces that look up printed, the rest the body's black), which the 32X's and Mega-CD's cards use too, and its three-button control pad (a smoothed outline with a rounded profile, its printing its own texture) with its 9-pin plug in CONTROL 1, three-quarter view, a node tree whose `focused` clip drops a cartridge (a made-up game, Odyssey, on its label) into the slot, pushing the flaps down, and seats it deep, its label's band out of sight, slides POWER on and lights the POWER light, plays on the D-pad, A, B and C, presses RESET, then switches off and takes the cartridge out, the flaps springing shut behind it; the pad and the clip are measured in `mega_drive_layout.py`) and `mega_drive_cartridge.py` the Mega Drive games' template (`models/templates/megadrive_cartridge.glb`: the `cover` slot above the label's band), with the printing and label crops `mega_drive_print.py` makes from `mega_drive_label.png` (and the card's Odyssey label and its pad's printing);

## The kits the later cards share

`card.py` (an atlas's UVs, materials, smoothing, a cable's curve, and `finish()`: the node tree, the clip, the fit and the export), `screens_print.py` (a made-up game's cover, title screen and frames at a machine's resolution and palette; text screens) and `handheld.py` (a device standing up facing the viewer: its shell from its face's outline, its buttons' outlines, its screens); a card whose screen is a video (the Game Boys', the Game Gear's, the PSP's, the C64's, Flash's, DOS's, Windows 3.x's, Windows 9x's and Windows') encodes it with `card_video.py` (`encode()`: its frames piped to ffmpeg as H.264 4:2:0, a key frame a second, no sound, as `amstrad_cpc_video.py` does).

Each of these cards has its script with its `_layout.py` and `_print.py` (`<script> <images> <out>`; the print scripts take `artifacts/made-up-games <out>`): `sufami.py` (the SuFami Turbo in a Super Famicom, with `snes_print.py sfc`'s print), `supergrafx.py`, `zxnext.py`, `msxturbor.py`, `sg1000.py`, `arcadia.py`, `astrocade.py`, `crvision.py`, `scv.py`, `pv1000.py`, `multivision.py`, `supracan.py`, `vsmile.py`, `uzebox.py`, `megaduck.py`, `gamate.py`, `gmaster.py`, `gamecom.py`, `arduboy.py`, `ngage.py`, `palm.py`, `lcdgames.py`, `android.py`, `j2me.py` and `symbian.py`; `dragon32.py <images> <kind> <out>` makes the Dragon 32 and the Tano Dragon (`tanodragon`), `naomi.py <images> <kind> <out>` NAOMI, NAOMI 2 and NAOMI GD-ROM (`naomi`, `naomi2`, `naomigd`), and `arcade.py`'s card `typex` is Taito's Egret II (cabinet `egret2`; the file installs as `type-x.glb`).

The computers come from one kit: `computer.py <images> <card> <out>` builds a card from its `<card>_layout.py` (`pc`, `pc88`, `x1`, `x68000`, `fm7`, `fmtowns`, `moto`, `to8`, `samcoupe`, `spectravideo`, `adam`, `coco`, `trs80`, the last installing as `trs-80.glb`: its blocks, keyboard rows, display, media, movers, lights and cables, the clip's keyframes; `computer_layout.py` says what one holds) and `computer_print.py <card> artifacts/made-up-games <out>` draws its print and screens.
