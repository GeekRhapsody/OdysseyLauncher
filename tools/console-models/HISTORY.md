# Console cards: history

The console theme's cards' build records, moved here from docs/ARCHITECTURE.md's decisions log on 2026-11-03: each card's construction, its clip, its fixes, and the conventions and tooling the cards share. Add a row here when you build or change a card. What each script builds now is in [README.md](README.md); decisions about the app and the theme format stay in [the decisions log](../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#decisions-log).

| 2026-10-03 | **The console theme's Mega Drive is rebuilt, and its games are on a cartridge of the theme's own** (`megadrive_cartridge`). Both come from Blender scripts in `tools/console-models/`, as the Game Gear does. The console (`mega_drive.py`: 4,354 triangles, 7 materials, 2 textures) has a three-quarter view built in, and a cartridge as its own node: at rest it's scaled to 0.001 inside the slot, and its `focused` clip (6 s, looping) has it appear above the slot, slide in, seat, stay, then come out and vanish. The cartridge template (`mega_drive_cartridge.py`: 488 triangles, 4 materials, 1 texture) shows each game's `cover` (default chain) in a window above the label's own band (the grid and the MEGA DRIVE logo), cut along the band's curve; the console's cartridge has the owner's whole label (`mega_drive_label.png`). | The owner's request, from reference photos and a label. A cartridge at rest would be in every unfocused card and add to the rest pose's bounds; shrunk inside the slot it's neither seen nor counted, and the clip starts and ends shrunk (A7: near the rest pose). It reappears above rather than being pulled back up, so the loop has no seam. The window shows the cover rather than the `label` kind: ScreenScraper's label scans already have the band, which would then show twice, and covers are what most libraries have. An editor A/B scroll of the Mega Drive's 252 games (console-test library, no art) against memory-card's clamshell: no hitches either way, scroll p99 19.5 against 20.0 ms, GPU 1.35 against 2.0 ms a frame (the band texture and twice the triangles). |
| 2026-10-03 | **The console theme's Windows card is a gaming PC and its monitor** (`windows.glb`, from `tools/console-models/windows_pc.py` and the two images `windows_pc_print.py` draws: 2,202 triangles, 3 materials, 2 textures). A glass-sided mid-tower (the motherboard, a dual-fan cooler, the graphics card and RAM, the shroud and the front fans' backs showing inside; a hexagonal mesh front over three fans with lit rings), and a 24-inch monitor showing a made-up desktop, arranged as the owner's reference photo has them, and seen head-on and level as in the photo, not turned to a three-quarter view like the consoles. The glass isn't modelled, so the side is open. It has no clips: the RGB lights are lit but still. | The owner's request. A three-quarter tilt didn't suit this set. An animation was dropped: the screen's (the Start menu opening a racing game) would have needed a node per screen state popped in front of the desktop, and the RGB lights' colour can't be animated, since clips drive node transforms and morph targets (A7) and `ModelConverter` bakes each material's base colour into the vertices when a model loads. The owner chose no animation over a workaround or a launcher feature. |
| 2026-10-03 | **The console theme's Switch card** (`switch.glb`, from `tools/console-models/switch.py` and the printing `switch_print.py` draws, sharing `switch_layout.py`'s measurements: 8,964 triangles, 6 materials, one 2048² texture holding the front and the back). | 2026-10-11 | **The console theme's Game Boy is rebuilt, and the Game Boy Color and Game Boy Advance get cards of their own** (`gb.glb`, `gbc.glb`, `gba.glb`, from `tools/console-models/game_boy.py` and the atlases `game_boy_print.py` draws, sharing `game_boy_layout.py`: 4,592, 4,312 and 4,108 triangles, 5 or 6 materials, one 1024² texture each). A grey DMG-01, a teal Game Boy Color and an indigo Game Boy Advance, turned to three-quarter views. Each is a node tree: a root holding the turn and the fit, the console, and as the console's children its cartridge (a made-up game, Odyssey, on its label), its screen switched on (the DMG's: its Nintendo logo) and its power light lit, all three shrunk to nothing inside the console at rest. The `focused` clip (7 s) brings the cartridge in differently on each: on the Game Boy it pops up, wiggles, turns and drops in, the console squashing under it, the logo scrolling down and the console hopping; on the Color it bonks off the top, wobbles, lines up and slides in, and the console shimmies; on the Advance it swoops in on a barrel roll, and the console nods and hops. Then the screen goes off and the cartridge jumps out and vanishes. The clip is one Blender action with a slot per part, which the exporter writes as one glTF animation. | The owner's request: the old `gb.glb` (5.4 MB, four 1024² textures) was too heavy; the three new models are about 250 KB each. A node tree needed no launcher change: `ModelConverter` already merges nodes with their parents' transforms for the rest mesh and keeps the tree for clips. Parenting the parts to the console lets it squash and hop with its cartridge and screen attached. One action per part would have exported as several clips named `focused`, of which the launcher plays only the first. The cartridges appear just above the slot, so the clip reaches about 40% above the console's height, as the Mega Drive's does. Each atlas region is padded with its own edge pixels, because the DMG's logo quad showed the case colour bleeding in at its edges. |
| 2026-10-11 | **Recently played's alarm clock is remade to match the Dreamcast menu**, from a 10 s capture the owner supplied, measured frame by frame (`tools/console-models/alarm_clock.py`: 4,338 triangles, 3 plain materials, no textures). The `focused` clip is now the menu's one-second loop instead of the made-up 6 s one: the clock rocks like a wheel, its lean swinging 30 degrees either way in step with its travel (0.58 radii either side, about what rolling gives), hopping 0.44 radii on the way right and 0.1 on the way back, keeping its round shape; its hands scissor, turning opposite ways once a second and crossing at twelve and six, and rest at ten to two, where the clip starts and ends; its bells pulse in turn, four times a second, one growing by a quarter as the other shrinks. The model follows the capture too: thick square hands widening to their tips with no cap, a fat round rim, golden domes for bells, each resting on the rim at one point (its own node, scaled from there), a stubby knob at three o'clock; turned a little from its right and its face tipped up 4 degrees, so that from the grid's camera it's seen head on, as in the menu. It's drawn at 85% of a full-size card: two specks (triangles a hundred-thousandth of its size, facing away, so culled) in front of it and behind it make its depth its largest side, which the launcher fits to 1 m. | The owner's request, with the video. The lean and the sideways travel were measured from the bells' and the knob's angles about the face and the body's centroid in each frame: both are near sine waves with the same phase and a 1 s period, and the travel per degree of lean is about the clock's radius, which is rolling. The hands' crossings, alternating at twelve and six every half second, fit two hands turning opposite ways at the same speed. The body's outline is 0.97 as tall as wide in every frame, so it doesn't squash (a first version did, from the face's outline, which the hands break up); the bells' pixel areas alternate about 2.4 to 1 with peaks every 7.5 frames at 30 fps. The owner's report on the first version: the bells clipped into the body, where the menu's just touch it, and the clock was a little large. A round card fills the 1 m fit both ways, larger than a wide console looks; the launcher has no per-card scale, and the specks give one without a manifest key. |
